The Beginning

My name is Koh Tze Rui, and I'm a Year 3 Computer Engineering student at the National University of Singapore. I always knew I wanted to be an engineer—drawn to hard problems and the intellectual high of figuring things out.

The Search

Working up to Year 2, I dabbled in many forms of Software Engineering: the classic SWE path with containerisation and abstraction, then quant with algorithm books and chasing the money. After two years of this during national service and into university, I just got bored and quietly stepped away from engineering in Year 1.

The Spark

Then a friend sent me a link to an HPC competition—build a single board computer cluster. I thought, why not? And the rest was history. My competitive personality kept me grinding through the night, chasing the last bit of performance in the system.

The Lesson

Sometimes you don't find your path by walking forward. You find it by getting bored enough to look sideways. Every dead end taught me something. Every detour shaped what I was actually looking for.

Keep chasing the problem that won't let you sleep.

A Closing Thought

I used to think engineers were the ones who built the most. Now I think they're the ones who refuse to stop until it's right.
